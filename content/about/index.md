My research interests are in computational physics and numerical methods: molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo simulation, inverse problems, and Bayesian inference (MCMC) for fitting and testing physical models against data, such as force-field parameterization. Applications in biological and materials physics, including how metal–peptide frameworks respond to water.

I am applying to physics PhD programs for Fall 2027.

## Current research

I am a volunteer researcher on two projects. At the American Museum of Natural History, I work on [Resolved Stellar Clusters](/research/resolved-stellar-clusters/) with Dave Zurek, auditing and correcting the statistical calculations in a code that assigns cluster-membership probabilities from Gaia astrometry. At the University of North Carolina at Charlotte, I work with Qiang Zhu's group on water-responsive metal–peptide frameworks, scoping a first simulation target.

## Training

I earned an M.S. in Astrophysics from the CUNY Graduate Center in 2025, advised by Lucy Lu. My thesis, [_Starspot Inference Using Light Curve Inversion Techniques_](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/gc_etds/6480/), tested what light-curve inversion can and cannot recover about stellar surfaces.

I started college at Baruch College in 2017 and completed a B.S. in Computational Astrophysics, cum laude, through the CUNY Baccalaureate for Unique and Interdisciplinary Studies, with Baruch as my home college.

From 2018 to 2022 I was an AstroCom NYC Scholar, in an NSF-supported CUNY research mentoring program run with the American Museum of Natural History and the Flatiron Institute. Through it I worked on [two-point statistics in simulations of the star-forming interstellar medium](/research/star-forming-ism/) with Chang-Goo Kim and on [stellar spectroscopy](/research/stellar-spectroscopy/) with Allyson Sheffield. I then joined the [Jhelum stellar stream](/research/jhelum-stellar-stream/) analysis through SDSS FAST, and mapped [brown-dwarf atmospheres](/research/brown-dwarf-atmospheres/) with Johanna Vos in the BDNYC group at AMNH.

## Teaching

In 2025–26 I was an adjunct lecturer at Baruch College. I was sole instructor for the introductory physics lecture (PHYS 2002), writing the lectures, exams, and quizzes, and lead instructor for the introductory and Physics II laboratories (PHYS 2003 and PHYS 3001), with about 25–30 students per class.
