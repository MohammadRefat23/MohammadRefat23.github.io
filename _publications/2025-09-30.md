## Overview

Stellar brightness varies as starspots and other surface features rotate into and out of view. Although these rotational light curves contain information about the distribution of stellar surface features, recovering a surface map from a one-dimensional brightness signal is a highly degenerate inverse problem.

This thesis investigated computational techniques for inferring stellar surface structure from rotational light curves. Synthetic light curves and surface maps were generated using forward-modeling tools, then analyzed with light-curve inversion methods to determine which properties of stellar activity could be reliably recovered from photometric observations.

</section>

<section class="project-section">

## Research Objectives

* Evaluate how accurately light-curve inversion techniques can recover the locations and properties of stellar surface features.

* Investigate how inclination, spot configuration, rotational sampling, and observational noise affect reconstructed surface maps.

* Compare inferred maps against known synthetic surface distributions generated through forward modeling.

* Identify which characteristics of stellar activity are constrained by rotational photometry and which remain fundamentally degenerate.

</section>

<section class="project-section">

## Key Findings

* Light-curve inversion can recover broad longitudinal structure and the rotational evolution of dominant stellar surface features under favorable observing conditions.

* Reconstructed latitude information is substantially more uncertain because different surface configurations can produce similar one-dimensional light curves.

* Stellar inclination, observational cadence, noise, and the number of active regions strongly influence reconstruction quality.

* Comparisons with synthetic surface maps demonstrate that inversion results should be interpreted as constrained representations of stellar activity rather than unique images of the stellar surface.

</section>

<section class="project-section">

## My Contributions

* Designed and implemented the computational workflow for generating, segmenting, fitting, and evaluating rotational light curves.

* Developed Python pipelines for analyzing synthetic and observed time-series data across multiple stellar rotations.

* Generated forward models and synthetic stellar surface configurations using tools including <code>starry</code>, <code>fleck</code>, and <code>butterpy</code>.

* Evaluated recovered surface maps against known input maps to quantify the strengths and limitations of the inversion process.

* Produced the figures, computational analysis, scientific interpretation, and written thesis.

</section>
