## Overview

The Jhelum stellar stream is a low-density structure in the inner halo of the Milky Way. Studying its stellar members, chemical composition, and orbital properties can help constrain the nature of its progenitor and provide insight into the assembly history of the Galaxy.

This study combined APOGEE-2 spectroscopy with Gaia DR2 astrometry to search for red giant stars associated with the stream. From 289 APOGEE-2 red giants observed in the direction of Jhelum, seven stars were identified as having astrometric properties consistent with the stream.

</section>

<section class="project-section">

## Key Findings

* Identified seven APOGEE-2 red giants with astrometric properties consistent with the Jhelum stellar stream.

* Found one new likely member near the tip of the red giant branch with a metallicity of approximately ([\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}] = -2.2).

* Determined that the orbital properties of Jhelum and Gaia–Enceladus–Sausage stars generally do not support a common origin.

* Found that the chemical abundances of confirmed Jhelum members are consistent with an accreted dwarf-galaxy progenitor, although a globular-cluster origin could not be ruled out.

</section>
